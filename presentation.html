The task: from raw 200 hertz accelerometer and gyroscope, predict the mean body-frame velocity of every one-second window. One model handles all four bodies, and at test time it isn't told which one it is looking at. The score mixes velocity error and a trajectory error over 20-metre segments, averaged per trajectory and then equally over platforms. Remember that averaging, because we shaped training around it.

Hack2Publish · TartanIMU 2026
Before training anything, we checked how much a window's neighbours know about it. Just averaging the true velocity of the two neighbouring windows cuts the error of predicting zero by about two thirds on car and dog. So the answer is mostly in the neighbourhood, and a per-window model throws it away. The test trajectories are given whole, so using that context is allowed. That decided the design.

Hack2Publish · TartanIMU 2026
The model reads a 16-second chunk of raw IMU. A convolution stem brings it down to 20 hertz tokens, eight dilated convolution blocks give about 13 seconds of receptive field, and a small three-layer transformer mixes the whole chunk. It outputs a velocity every 50 milliseconds, and we average those within each window. We also add a few strap-down physics features, like the integrated gyro orientation. One weight set, no platform input.
Training & inference

Train the way the metric scores

Loss
Window error + drift error
A Huber loss for velocity (AVE) plus a loss on the accumulated drift that ATE20 sees.
Sampling
Platform → trajectory → chunk
Uniform at each step, like the metric. A short drone clip counts as much as a long drive.
Augmentation
Respects the physics
Random re-mounting, time dilation and sensor bias, applied to the label too.
Inference
8 overlapping chunks per window
Stride 2, centre-weighted average. Nothing else: no TTA, no smoothing.
Hack2Publish · TartanIMU 2026
Training follows the metric. The loss has a window velocity term and a drift term on the integrated error, which is what the trajectory metric measures. We sample a platform uniformly, then a trajectory uniformly, then a chunk, exactly how the metric averages. Augmentation is physical: random re-mounting rotations, time dilation and sensor bias, applied to the label too. At test time each window is covered by eight overlapping chunks, and we trust the chunk where it sits near the centre most.

Hack2Publish · TartanIMU 2026
The drone data comes from two sources. Drone-B has its gyro z axis inverted relative to its own ground truth. The network copes, because that source is easy to recognise, and its error per flight sits between 0.16 and 0.43 metres per second. Drone-A, the racing drone, is recorded twice per flight by two IMUs, and even after the best alignment their velocity labels disagree by up to 0.9 metres per second. Its error stayed high no matter what we changed. All of this is measured on train and val only.

Hack2Publish · TartanIMU 2026
A practical lesson. Training the identical recipe on two GPUs moved the public score by 0.018, more than most of our real improvements. The public split is dominated by a few short racing-drone clips. So we decided everything on validation with the organisers' exact scorer. Val tracked the full test well: 0.198 against 0.219. The public split gave 0.286 and the final private board 0.161; each board uses about half of the test data.
Takeaways

One universal inertial model works. The bottleneck is the data.

Context wins
Reading 16 s at once gave most of the gain. No negative transfer between bodies.
Next for us
Estimate bias, gravity and time offset explicitly. Build a causal, real-time version.
For the benchmark
Document frame conventions and sync per source. Declare the duplicate racing recordings.
Hack2Publish · TartanIMU 2026
To sum up. A single universal inertial model works: five to seven centimetres per second on three very different bodies, with no platform label and no negative transfer. Most of that came from reading whole trajectories. Next, we'd estimate bias, gravity and time offset explicitly and build a causal version for real-time use. For the benchmark, we'd suggest documenting frame conventions and synchronisation per source, and declaring the duplicate racing recordings.

Backup · likely questions

Questions we expect

vs RoNIN, TLIO, AirIO?
They regress from 1–2 s windows or fuse in an EKF. We predict a whole 16 s span and average overlaps.
vs Tartan IMU baseline?
It uses per-platform heads. The challenge forbids routing, so we have none.
Real time?
No. It is offline and uses future samples. A causal version needs a different design.
How reliable?
Seed 0, single runs. Same-recipe reruns moved the public score by up to 0.018, the private by 0.005.
Test labels?
Never used. Drone findings come from train and val files only.
Why not ensemble?
Ranked entries had to be one weight set. Ensemble: 0.2171 on val, never ranked.
